# Building a FOSS Rock, Mineral & Gem Identification App: A Feasibility-to-Build Report

## TL;DR
- **Build it as a hybrid, offline-first, FOSS Android app where the guided diagnostic key (streak, Mohs hardness, luster, specific gravity, cleavage, acid test) is the primary, scientifically-defensible engine, and photo ML classification is a clearly-labeled "suggestion only" helper** — because the peer-reviewed literature shows photo-only ID of *raw hand specimens* is unreliable (even human experts cannot distinguish many species by eye), while lab/microscope/spectroscopy conditions inflate the headline "98% accuracy" numbers that do not transfer to a user's phone photo of a dirty rock.
- **A non-developer can realistically ship a working v1 in roughly 4–9 months** using AI-assisted coding (Claude Code/Cursor) plus Flutter or a PWA, TensorFlow Lite/LiteRT for on-device inference, and transfer learning from MobileNet — but the honest bottleneck is *data licensing and long-term maintenance*, not the code. Mindat's data is non-commercial/share-alike and its photos are individually copyrighted; build your diagnostic property database from genuinely open sources (RRUFF, IMA list, open property tables) and train image models only on openly-licensed datasets.
- **The MLM/political-economy framing is analytically apt but should not be treated as automatically correct**: subscription apps (e.g., Glority's Rock Identifier at ~$29.99–$39.99/yr) do enclose and rent-seek on what is largely public-domain, publicly-funded mineralogical knowledge — Glority reached an estimated $173M ARR — and a FOSS app is a genuine instance of commons-based peer production (Benkler). But FOSS carries its own contradictions (unpaid maintainer labor, corporate capture, dependence on Google/Apple infrastructure) that a serious analysis must not romanticize.

## Key Findings

**1. The competitive landscape is dominated by proprietary, subscription-gated "snap-a-photo" apps with well-documented accuracy complaints, leaving a real gap for a FOSS, test-driven tool.**
- The market leaders — Rock Identifier: Stone ID, Rock ID, Stone Identifier — are proprietary and monetized through aggressive auto-renewing subscriptions: roughly $3.99–$6.99/week, ~$9.99/month, or ~$29.99–$39.99/year, sometimes with a $74.99 "lifetime" tier. Free use is typically capped (three IDs/day or a 7-day trial).
- **Rock Identifier: Stone ID is a product of Glority Global Group Ltd.**, a Hangzhou-based AI computer-vision company whose portfolio (PictureThis, Picture Insect, Picture Bird, Picture Fish, Rock Identifier) monetizes the same "identify-by-photo-then-subscribe" model. Per AppBrain, Glority has been "active since 2017" and its five-app portfolio "has in total collected about 100 million installs and 1 million ratings," with PictureThis its flagship. Glority's Rock Identifier is priced at $29.99/year (RockHounding.org's January 2026 field test), and Play Store reviewers report the annual price rising to ~$40; the sibling app Stone Identifier Rock ID lists "Premium Yearly $39.99/year... Premium Weekly $3.99/week."
- **The category generates very large recurring revenue.** Per Unique Research's 2025 ranking of the top 100 private AI companies by annual recurring revenue (cited in Tech Buzz China, "The State of Chinese AI Apps 2025"), Glority — "creator of the PictureThis plant-identification app" — reached an estimated **$173 million ARR (No. 20 globally)**, making it the largest of the four Chinese firms on that list. This is the financial scale of the enclosure a FOSS alternative would push against.
- User reviews repeatedly report misidentifications ("It misidentified several of my stones but gets the basic ones everyone already knows"; obsidian and petrified wood both returned as "lava stone") and resentment at subscriptions for what users see as static, public-domain content ("this is a database for collected rocks... I can do [it] on my own with google images, mindat, and the notes app").
- **FOSS/free precedents for the test-based approach exist but are narrow.** RockCheck / KamenCheck (a Slovenian educational project) is free, works via a dichotomous yes/no key, and includes a "School of Rock" teaching module — but it is rock-focused, Android-only, and not a full FOSS mineral-species identifier with ML. Rockd (UW-Madison Macrostrat) is free and geology-focused. No widely-distributed app currently combines (a) FOSS licensing, (b) on-device photo ML, and (c) a rigorous guided diagnostic key for mineral species — that is the genuine gap.

**2. The peer-reviewed literature shows photo-only mineral ID is fundamentally limited; the impressive accuracy numbers come from lab/spectroscopy/microscopy conditions that do not transfer to phone snapshots of raw specimens.**
- Very high reported accuracies exist but are conditional: a 1D-CNN on Raman spectra hit **98.37%** (Rock Classification through Knowledge-Enhanced Deep Learning, arXiv 2510.13937); **MobileNetV2 reached 98.98%** on a curated 7-mineral set (MDPI, *Applied Sciences* 2025); an **Xception model reached 98%** on 3,179 microscope images across 40 mineral types (ScienceDirect 2025); hyperspectral CNNs (mineral-ResNet) reached ~92%. **These use spectroscopy, thin-sections under cross-polarized light, microscopy, or small curated class sets — not a hobbyist's photo.**
- The most honest benchmark for the actual use case is **MineralImage5k** (Nesteruk et al., *Computers & Geosciences*, 2023), built on the Fersman Mineralogical Museum collection (>5,000 classes, ~44,000 images). It states verbatim: *"Mineral image recognition is a challenging computer vision problem. Without external tools, even a human expert cannot distinguish some mineral species accurately,"* citing "high interclass variability" and severe shortage of labeled raw-specimen data. A multilevel-feature study reached only **88% on 22 species**; a transfer-learning VGG16 on 7 minerals reached **83%**.
- The mineralogy pedagogy is unambiguous that **hand-specimen ID requires physical tests, because color is unreliable** (hematite appears silver, black, or red but always gives a red-brown streak; pyrite is brassy but streaks greenish-black). Standard practice is a decision chain — luster → hardness → streak → cleavage → specific gravity → special tests (acid fizz, magnetism) — i.e., exactly a dichotomous key. Polymorphs (same chemistry, different structure) can be indistinguishable even in hand sample and need XRD. This is the scientific case for making the guided key primary and the photo a hint.

**3. Open datasets and property databases exist, but licensing is the single biggest constraint and must drive your design.**
- **Mindat** is the largest database, but its front-end data is *not* freely reusable at scale: the OpenMindat API is governed by a **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (a tailored non-commercial, share-alike fork), photos remain the individual copyright of thousands of contributing photographers, and Mindat explicitly restricts bulk copying, commercial use, and scraping (with a stated lifetime-ban risk for scrapers). You can use OpenMindat data for a non-commercial FOSS app *with attribution and share-alike*, but you cannot ship Mindat photos, cannot relicense the data permissively, and it collides with GPL/MIT purity and any commercial App-Store posture. The OpenMindat *R package* code is MIT-licensed, but that is the client wrapper, not the data.
- **RRUFF** (University of Arizona) is the gold-standard open reference: downloadable Raman spectra, XRD, chemistry, cell parameters, and sample photos as zip files with a citation requirement — excellent for building structured property records (and future spectral features); check photo reuse terms per record.
- **The IMA-approved mineral list** (accessible via Mindat/RRUFF) is the canonical species taxonomy backbone.
- **Image training data**: Kaggle hosts several usable sets — "Minerals Identification Dataset" (7 classes, hand specimens), "Mineral photos" (~39,000 images / 15 categories), "Gemstones Images" (87 gemstone classes), plus a "Comprehensive database of Minerals" (3,112 minerals with composition/properties as a structured table). MineralImage5k (Kaggle/HuggingFace, from Sber AI/MSU) is the most scientifically-serious raw-specimen set. **Check each dataset's license individually** — Kaggle sets range from CC0 to CC-BY to unspecified/"all rights reserved," and unlicensed sets are legally unsafe to redistribute inside a FOSS app.
- **iNaturalist** demonstrates the model but withholds the crown jewels: its software is open source, but its *full* computer-vision model is deliberately kept private. Verbatim from the `inatVisionAPI` repo: *"iNaturalist makes a subset of its machine learning models publicly available while keeping full species classification models private due to intellectual property considerations and organizational policy related to all-rights-reserved photos... We provide 'small' models trained on approximately 500 taxa."* You will need to train your own model.

**4. A non-developer can build this; the realistic path is AI-assisted development + a cross-platform framework + on-device ML + a hand-authored diagnostic key.**
- **Frameworks**: Flutter (Dart) leads for on-device ML and single-codebase mobile; React Native (JavaScript) has the largest ecosystem and the strongest AI-tooling support (Copilot/Claude/Cursor are trained on far more JS). Seek by iNaturalist and the original Merlin used React Native. A Progressive Web App (PWA) is the lowest-barrier start (no app-store gatekeeping, trivial hosting) but has weaker offline ML and camera integration. For a non-developer leaning on AI assistants, React Native or Flutter are both viable; a PWA is the fastest to a working prototype.
- **On-device ML**: TensorFlow Lite / LiteRT (or ONNX / Core ML) runs quantized MobileNet models at 3.7–80ms inference and 0.5–3.4MB size — fully offline, matching field use and privacy. **Transfer learning** (freeze a MobileNetV2/EfficientNet ImageNet base, retrain the top layers) is the standard approach and needs only "hundreds to thousands of images per label," not millions. Expect meaningful results for maybe 20–50 common species; expect the model to degrade badly on rare species and raw/weathered specimens.
- **The guided diagnostic key** is a software problem, not an ML problem: it is a decision tree / rules engine over a structured property table (hardness range, streak color, luster, cleavage, SG, magnetism, acid reaction, crystal system). This is where you get real scientific validity — it is fully offline, fully explainable, and can be hand-authored from open property data plus a mineralogy textbook. Build this first.
- **Realistic timeline for a motivated non-developer**: ~2–3 months to become comfortable with the framework (industry estimates put beginners at 1–2 months for React Native/JS, 2–3 months for Flutter/Dart) with AI help; a working diagnostic-key MVP ~1–3 months after that; a photo-ML feature in a further ~2–3 months (most of that is data collection/cleaning, not coding). Total ~4–9 months to a credible v1.

**5. FOSS ecosystem specifics create concrete, decidable choices — and one hard Apple problem.**
- **License**: For maximum "commons" protection use **GPLv3 or AGPLv3** (AGPL if any server component, to close the SaaS loophole); for maximum reuse/adoption use **MIT/Apache-2.0**. Note that **GPL and Apple's App Store terms are legally incompatible** — the FSF's 2010 GNU Go enforcement case established that Apple's Usage Rules impose "further restrictions" the GPL forbids (Apple pulled GNU Go rather than comply). Practical resolutions: (a) distribute on **F-Droid** and via direct APK, where GPL is welcome; (b) use a permissive license (MIT/Apache) if you want the Apple App Store; or (c) dual-license (GPL for the community + a separate App Store distribution license you grant as sole copyright holder).
- **Distribution**: F-Droid is the natural FOSS home (builds from source, flags "anti-features," but has a smaller catalogue/audience and a more manual update flow). Google Play reaches the mass Android audience and tolerates GPL. Apple's App Store requires a permissive license or dual-licensing.
- **Hosting costs**: An offline-first design (on-device model + bundled property DB) can run at **essentially zero server cost**. Costs appear only if you add crowd-sourced uploads, a shared observations database, or server-side inference. Merlin, Seek, and Pl@ntNet all ship on-device models precisely to work offline.
- **Precedents to model after**: **Seek by iNaturalist** is open source (React Native, MIT license, repo `inaturalist/SeekReactNative`) and runs an on-device model offline — the closest architectural template. **Merlin Bird ID** (Cornell Lab / Visipedia) is free, on-device, ~90% accurate on good photos (per Colby College/Maine Birds and Cornell), draws on training sets of millions of photos/sounds in the Macaulay Library, covers 10,000+ species, and combines ML with a question-based key and range/season priors — the closest UX template. **Pl@ntNet** is free with an open backend/dataset (CC-BY-SA data) though its app code is only partly open. All three succeed by pairing ML *suggestions* with human confirmation and contextual priors — the exact posture your app should adopt.

**6. Through an MLM/political-economy lens: the app is a legitimate act of decommodifying enclosed knowledge, but FOSS is not automatically outside capital.**
- **Enclosure and rent-seeking**: Mineralogical facts (hardness, streak, chemistry) are public-domain scientific knowledge, much of it produced by publicly-funded universities and museums (RRUFF/Arizona, Mindat/Hudson Institute nonprofit, NSF-funded OpenMindat, the Fersman Museum). Subscription apps enclose this commons behind paywalls and extract recurring rent (~$30–40/yr; Glority ~$173M ARR) for repackaging it — a direct parallel to the Marxist critique of academic publishers who "extract profit from research communities by enclosing and restricting access to publicly funded scientific knowledge commons" (triple-C literature on knowledge enclosure and "accumulation by dispossession").
- **FOSS as a mode of production**: Benkler's *commons-based peer production* names exactly this — non-market, non-proprietary, self-selected cooperative production of information goods (Linux, Wikipedia, OSM), which he argues can be "a more cost-effective institutional form than either markets or hierarchical organizations" when physical capital (computers) is widely distributed. A FOSS mineral-ID app decommodifies the identification service and returns it to the commons; this fits an MLM emphasis on socializing the means of (knowledge) production and privileging use-value over exchange-value.
- **Contradictions the framework itself exposes**: (a) *Unpaid labor* — FOSS relies on volunteer maintainer labor that firms can capture as surplus (Benkler's own distinction between commons-based peer production and "firm-hosted peer production" like Yelp/TripAdvisor, where governance rests on proprietary control of the platform). (b) *Corporate capture* — the toolchain (Android/AOSP, TensorFlow, Kaggle, GitHub, Colab) is Google/Microsoft-owned; "free" tooling disciplines the commons toward corporate platforms. (c) *Residual enclosure of data* — even "open" data carries restrictions (Mindat's NC-SA, all-rights-reserved photos), and iNaturalist keeps its best model private for IP reasons, showing the commons is partial and contested even among nominal allies. A rigorous MLM reading treats the app not as a *solution* to enclosure but as a small counter-hegemonic practice whose limits (platform dependence, maintainer burnout, the difficulty of scaling non-market production against a $173M-ARR competitor) are structural, not incidental. The framework is illuminating here — but it is interpretive social science, contestable rather than settled, and should be held as an analytical lens, not a proof.

## Details

### Scientific validity — setting honest accuracy expectations
The "98%+" figures are real but come from controlled conditions: Raman/hyperspectral spectroscopy, thin-sections under cross-polarized light, or microscope images of clean broken surfaces, usually on small curated class sets (7–40 classes). The moment you move to a phone photo of a raw, dirty, weathered specimen with variable lighting, accuracy collapses — MineralImage5k exists precisely because this raw-specimen problem was unbenchmarked and hard, and its authors state plainly that even human experts fail on many species by eye. Design implications: (1) never present a single confident answer — show a ranked shortlist with confidence; (2) always route the user into the diagnostic key to confirm; (3) scope the ML to a modest set of common, visually-distinct species; (4) label ML output "suggestion — confirm with tests."

### Datasets & licensing — a decision table
- **Property/text data (safe for a FOSS diagnostic key)**: RRUFF (downloadable, cite), IMA mineral list, open Kaggle property tables (check license), webmineral-style property fields. Facts are not copyrightable, though database *compilations* can be — prefer explicitly-licensed sources and attribute.
- **Mindat/OpenMindat**: usable non-commercially with CC BY-NC-SA attribution + share-alike; **do not** ship its photos; **do not** relicense permissively; incompatible with a permissive-MIT or commercial-App-Store posture. Best used as a reference/enrichment source, not a bundled dataset.
- **Image training data**: prefer CC0/CC-BY Kaggle sets, MineralImage5k (check terms), and your own photographed specimens. Avoid scraping Mindat/Google Images (copyright + Mindat's explicit anti-scraping/ban stance).

### Technical build path — recommended stack
1. **Diagnostic key first**: author a structured mineral property table (start with ~50–100 common species) + a decision-tree engine + free-text/filter search. Fully offline. This alone is a useful, scientifically-valid app.
2. **Add on-device photo ML second**: transfer-learn MobileNetV2/EfficientNet-Lite in Python (Google Colab, free GPU) using TFLite Model Maker; export a quantized `.tflite`; bundle it. Scope to common species; show ranked suggestions feeding into the key.
3. **Framework**: Flutter (best on-device ML ergonomics) or React Native (best AI-assist tooling, matches Seek). PWA if you want the fastest prototype and can accept weaker offline/camera.
4. **AI-assisted coding**: Claude Code / Cursor / Copilot to scaffold screens, wire the TFLite plugin, and generate the decision-tree UI. Expect to *learn while building*, not avoid learning.

### FOSS distribution & licensing decision
- Commons-maximal path → **GPLv3/AGPLv3**, ship on **F-Droid** + direct APK, skip/defer the Apple App Store.
- Maximum reach including iPhone → **MIT/Apache-2.0**, ship everywhere, accept weaker copyleft.
- Both → dual-license as sole copyright holder.
- Keep it offline-first to keep hosting at ~$0.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (months 0–3) — Ship the diagnostic key as a standalone FOSS app.**
- Build a structured property database for ~50–100 common minerals/rocks/gems from RRUFF + IMA list + open property tables, with attribution.
- Implement a dichotomous/decision-tree key (luster → hardness → streak → cleavage → SG → special tests) plus filtered search.
- Use Flutter or a PWA, AI-assisted. License GPLv3, publish on GitHub + F-Droid.
- **Benchmark to proceed**: the key reliably narrows common specimens to a correct shortlist in your own testing; positive feedback from a handful of rockhound/geology-student testers.

**Stage 2 (months 3–6) — Add on-device photo ML as a labeled "suggestion" layer.**
- Curate an openly-licensed image set for ~20–50 common, visually-distinct species; transfer-learn MobileNet; export quantized TFLite; bundle offline.
- Present ranked suggestions with confidence, always routing to the key to confirm.
- **Benchmark to proceed / re-scope**: if top-3 accuracy on *your own held-out raw-specimen photos* is not clearly useful (roughly ≥70–80% top-3 on the common set), keep the ML narrow or defer it. If raw-specimen accuracy stays low, that is expected per the literature — lean harder on the key.

**Stage 3 (months 6–9+) — Community & sustainability.**
- Open contribution: let users submit openly-licensed specimen photos (with explicit CC consent) to grow the training set — the iNaturalist/Pl@ntNet commons model.
- Add locality/context priors (like Merlin's range/season) if you add a data backend; otherwise stay offline.
- Address the maintenance-burden risk explicitly: recruit 1–2 co-maintainers early, document everything, keep the architecture simple. A solo non-developer maintaining an ML app long-term is the highest project risk.
- **Reconsider server components** only if community uploads justify the hosting cost and moderation labor.

**Cross-cutting**: Do not build on Mindat photos or scraped images; do not relicense NC-SA data permissively; do not market accuracy you cannot demonstrate on raw specimens.

## Caveats
- **The accuracy ceiling is real and structural**: photo-only ID of raw hand specimens is inherently unreliable per the peer-reviewed literature; treat any app's photo output (yours or competitors') as a hint, not a determination. This is the single most important honesty constraint.
- **Data licensing is the binding constraint**, not coding difficulty. Mindat's NC-SA terms and individually-copyrighted photos, plus unlicensed Kaggle sets, can quietly make a "FOSS" app non-compliant. Audit every data source's license.
- **Maintenance burden for a solo non-developer is the top delivery risk** — ML apps rot (OS updates, dependency churn, model drift). Plan for co-maintainers.
- **Apple + GPL is genuinely incompatible**; choose your license and distribution channel deliberately up front.
- **Corporate financial figures are estimates**: Glority's $173M ARR (Unique Research via Tech Buzz China) and the ~100M-installs portfolio figure (AppBrain) are third-party estimates, not audited disclosures; treat them as indicative of scale, not precise.
- **The MLM framing is applied analytically, not assumed**: it illuminates enclosure and commons dynamics well, but FOSS's own contradictions (unpaid labor, platform capture) mean a FOSS app is a partial, contested counter-practice — not an escape from capital. That section rests partly on interpretive social-science literature, which is contestable rather than settled fact.
